# What the company still owes

Call `get_legal_status`. It returns the entity, the states, how far the
registration checklist has got, and the recurring filings with their next
due dates and day counts.

## Lead with the date

A founder asking "what do I owe" wants to know what is due soonest and
what happens if they miss it, in that order. Both are in the tool's
answer; the amount matters less than the date, because the date is the
part that cannot be fixed afterwards.

Say the consequence when there is one. "Delaware annual report, due
1 March, 128 days away" is useful. "Delaware annual report, due 1 March,
and missing it costs $200 plus 1.5% a month and eventually good standing"
is the sentence that makes someone diarise it.

## Two states means two sets of filings

Incorporating in Delaware and operating in California means the company
owes both. Founders routinely expect only the first. If the status shows
an operating state different from the state of incorporation, say so
plainly rather than listing the obligations and leaving them to notice.

## The California first year is exempt, and this surprises people

A corporation that incorporates **or qualifies** in California on or after
1 January 2000 is exempt from the $800 minimum franchise tax for its
**first** taxable year — it pays tax measured on California net income
instead, which is close to nothing before revenue. The $800 floor starts
in the second year, and it is not prorated for a short year.

Sercle's own answer carries this. Repeat it when the $800 comes up,
because the widespread belief is the opposite and acting on the belief
means paying money that is not owed.

## A date that is not set yet

Some filings are anchored to the day the company registered in the
operating state, so they have no date until that checklist step is ticked.
When the tool says so, tell the founder which step to tick and that the
tick date is what sets the deadlines — not a detail to skip, since
ticking it on the wrong day moves real dates.

## You are not their accountant

Amounts drift and every one of these is linked to the state's own page.
Point at the source rather than asserting a figure from memory, and say
plainly that filing deadlines and amounts should be confirmed with the
state or an accountant before money moves.
